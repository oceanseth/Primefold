# Primefold

An interactive **base-fractal prime scope**, plus a short expository paper on what we found
when we pointed it at the twin prime conjecture.

![Primefold rendering the twindragon: 262,144 integers in base β = −1+i, composites as
blue-grey dust, primes amber, twin primes rose](assets/preview.png)

**Live scope:** open `index.html` in any browser (no build, no dependencies), or visit the
GitHub Pages deployment of this repo.

## What it is

Every integer `n` is written in an ordinary digit base D, then its digits are re-priced in a
complex base β: `z(n) = Σ dₖ·βᵏ`. Composites render as faint dust, primes glow amber, twin
primes burn rose. Drag β through the complex plane and the prime constellation deforms through
the twindragon (β = −1+i), Knuth's quater-imaginary system (β = 2i), and everything between.

The instrument includes:

- a draggable complex-base picker with presets (twindragon, quater-imaginary, π·i, golden φ…),
- Hilbert-curve and Ulam-spiral embeddings for comparison,
- a **twin-alignment score** R (circular statistics on the direction from z(p) to z(p+2)),
- a **β-scan** that sweeps β = |β|·e^(iθ) and charts R, hunting angles where twins line up,
- a direction-histogram rose plot with the mean-direction needle.

## The paper

[`paper/primefold-note.pdf`](paper/primefold-note.pdf) — *Twin pairs of Friedlander–Iwaniec
form: a census to 10⁷, a bi-norm decomposition, and a decoded coincidence.*

Short version: among primes p = a²+b⁴ (the Friedlander–Iwaniec form) with p+2 also prime,

1. every pair is a **bi-norm pair** — p is a norm from ℚ(i), and p+2 is forced by its residue
   mod 8 to be a norm from ℚ(√−2) or ℚ(√2), never ℚ(i);
2. exactly one exact relation links the two coordinate systems: the family
   **p = a²+16, p+2 = a²+2·3²**, generated by the coincidence 4²+2 = 2·3², provably unique of
   its kind via the quartic Pell equation y² = 8c⁴+1;
3. the family has 68 members below 10⁷ against a Hardy–Littlewood prediction of **67.9**;
4. a wrong-partner control shows the two parameterizations are otherwise statistically
   independent at this scale — structure below the parity barrier is exactly congruences plus
   one diophantine accident.

**Version 2 additions** (`experiments/beyond.py`):

5. census extended to **10⁹** (322,109 FI primes, 29,803 FI twins);
6. the **complete pass-through fold classification** b⁴+s = m·y² for shifts s ≤ 100 — 36
   folds, exactly two at the twin shift s = 2, revealing a second family
   **(a²+1, a²+3)** alongside (a²+16, a²+18); both confirmed against Hardy–Littlewood at
   10⁹ (278 vs 283.5 predicted; 374 vs 365.4);
7. first data on **gaps between FI primes themselves**: minimum gap 4, first at (37, 41);
   28,262 consecutive pairs within distance 246 below 10⁹ — an analogue of the
   Zhang–Maynard–Polymath "246" statement that no current theorem covers, since FI primes
   have relative density zero among primes. We record the data and pose the question.

Nothing in the note is deep, and it claims no novelty — it documents the observations, the
quantitative checks, and the honest negative result, with reproducible code.

## Reproduce

```
python3 experiments/census.py     # ~2 min: census to 10^7, family decode, controls
python3 experiments/beyond.py    # ~3 min: census to 10^9, fold table, FI gap statistics
```

Reference outputs are saved in `experiments/results.txt` and `experiments/results-beyond.txt`.

